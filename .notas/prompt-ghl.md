Build a landing page that sells GoHighLevel implementation services.

## Who this is for

emmvi (always lowercase, never "Emmvi") builds websites and the automation
behind them. This page sells the GoHighLevel side of that work to two readers:

1. **Agencies** that resell GHL and need someone to build inside it, under
   their brand.
2. **Businesses** already paying for a GHL licence they barely use.

Write for a busy, non-technical reader on a phone. Short sentences. No
consultant language.

## The positioning, and the hard part

emmvi has **five years of hands-on GoHighLevel work** and is **not** a
certified HighLevel partner.

Do not hide this and do not apologise for it. Address it head-on, once,
somewhere sensible — a FAQ entry is ideal. The argument to make: a badge says
you sat an exam; what matters is what runs in the account afterwards. Then
prove it by being specific about the work, which is what the rest of the page
does.

This is the page's edge. Every competitor for this search term leads with
"Certified". Leading with demonstrable specifics instead is the differentiator
— so the writing has to actually be specific.

## Hard rules — these are not style preferences

- **"emmvi", lowercase, always.** Even at the start of a sentence or a title.
- **Never state team size.** Not "our team of X", not "we are a small team".
  Naming cities is fine; naming a number is not.
- **No invented metrics.** No "48-hour delivery", no "60% less admin", no
  "100% completion rate", no "100+ accounts". If it was not measured, it does
  not go on the page. The only number allowed is the five years.
- **No percentage-of-revenue claims.** Ever.
- **No pricing.** It is not decided yet. Drive to a conversation instead.
- **No borrowed proof.** No logos, testimonials or case studies unless
  supplied. Leave a visible placeholder rather than inventing one.
- **Timezones: Europe and the Americas only.** emmvi works from Valencia and
  from Argentina. Do not claim Australia or Asia-Pacific coverage.
- Write "quote request", not "enquiry".

## What NOT to do

The obvious competitors for this term all look the same: a gold "Certified"
badge, a wall of round numbers, an hourly rate, and testimonials from agencies
nobody can verify. Do not produce that page. Specifically avoid:

- Trophy/badge iconography implying accreditation.
- Stat strips of unverifiable round numbers.
- "We don't just build — we guide" and similar empty contrast lines.
- A feature dump of GoHighLevel's own capabilities. The reader can get that
  from HighLevel. Sell what emmvi does *inside* it.

## Structure to aim for

1. Hero: the problem, not the product. Something closer to "you are paying for
   this and it is not running anything" than "GoHighLevel Expert Services".
2. What we build inside your account — the grouped list above, made concrete.
3. One worked example, shown in detail: a single workflow from trigger to
   outcome. Depth beats breadth here.
4. For agencies: white label, NDA, onboarding.
5. Migration, as its own block — it is the biggest objection and the biggest job.
6. How it works: look at the account → build → test → it runs.
7. What we don't do. Keep this. It is the most credible section on the page.
8. FAQ, including the certification question answered plainly.
9. Close: ask to see the account, not to "book a strategy call".

## Design

Match the existing emmvi site.

Typeface: DM Sans.

    --color-paper:        #ffffff
    --color-paper-alt:    #f8f8f8   section bands
    --color-paper-panel:  #f9fafd   large panels, radius 32
    --color-ink:          #171717   headings and primary text
    --color-ink-soft:     #666666   body copy
    --color-line:         #eaeaea   hairlines
    --color-violet:       #423af4   brand accent, used sparingly
    --night: linear-gradient(180deg, #171717 0%, #000000 62%, #2e2e2e 100%)

Light page, generous whitespace, hairline rules rather than heavy cards. One
dark `--night` band at most, for contrast. Violet is an accent — links, an open
FAQ question, a small mark — never a field of colour.

Body copy at 45–75 characters per line. Headings in sentence case.

Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA. Text contrast 4.5:1 minimum, interactive targets
24×24px minimum.
